Modelling Motor Protein Motion

Stochastic Dynamics and Directed Transport

S. Blom · L. Bekkely · K. Myhre

01

Introduction

Motor proteins play an important role in transporting molecular cargo within cells. Their ability to produce directed motion from thermal fluctuations makes them an interesting example of stochastic processes in biological systems.

In this project, we investigate how directed motion can emerge from random walks in asymmetric potential landscapes. By modelling the movement of particles under different potentials and temperatures, we study how thermal fluctuations and potential asymmetry influence the resulting particle dynamics.

Main question

How can stochastic thermal motion produce directed transport when interacting with an asymmetric potential landscape?

02

Diffusion and stochastic motion

We begin with the diffusion equation. For an initial distribution $h(x)$, the solution can be written using the Gaussian heat kernel:

$$ \phi(x,t)= \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(y) \exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^2}{4Dt}\right)\,dy $$

This solution satisfies

$$ \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} = D\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2}. $$

As $t\rightarrow0$, the Gaussian kernel approaches a Dirac delta distribution, giving

$$ \phi(x,t)\rightarrow h(x). $$
03

Particles in potential landscapes

At thermal equilibrium, the probability of finding a particle at position $x$ follows the Boltzmann distribution:

$$ P(x)=\frac{e^{-\beta V(x)}}{Z}, \qquad \beta=\frac{1}{k_BT}. $$

Here, $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant and $T$ is the temperature. The parameter $\beta$ therefore controls the relative influence of the potential and thermal fluctuations.

High temperature

Thermal fluctuations dominate and particles behave more like unbiased random walkers.

Low temperature

The potential becomes more important and particles are more strongly influenced by potential minima and barriers.

04

Random walks

The stochastic dynamics are implemented as a discrete random walk. At each time step, a particle can move left, remain stationary, or move right.

$$ p^+= \frac{e^{-\beta V(x+1)}} {e^{-\beta V(x-1)} +e^{-\beta V(x)} +e^{-\beta V(x+1)}} $$
$$ p^-= \frac{e^{-\beta V(x-1)}} {e^{-\beta V(x-1)} +e^{-\beta V(x)} +e^{-\beta V(x+1)}} $$
$$ p^0=1-p^+-p^-. $$

For a constant potential, all three probabilities are equal, producing an unbiased random walk.

From random walks to directed transport

The flashing ratchet alternates between a flat potential, where particles diffuse freely, and an asymmetric periodic potential.

Repeated switching can produce a net displacement even though the underlying particle motion is stochastic.

08

Asymmetry and current

The average particle current is measured from the difference between rightward and leftward transitions:

$$ J=\frac{n^+-n^-}{N_p}. $$
α < 0.5

Positive current.

α = 0.5

Approximately zero current.

α > 0.5

Negative current.

09

Effect of the flashing interval

The duration of each flashing phase influences the resulting particle current. Intermediate flashing intervals can produce stronger directed transport than very short or very long intervals.

$$ J_{\mathrm{avg}} = \frac{N_x}{4T_p} \left[ \operatorname{erfc} \left( \frac{\alpha N_x}{2} \sqrt{\frac{3}{T_p}} \right) - \operatorname{erfc} \left( \frac{(1-\alpha)N_x}{2} \sqrt{\frac{3}{T_p}} \right) \right]. $$
10

Numerical and analytical comparison

Numerical simulations were compared with the analytical expression for the average current. The two approaches show agreement over a range of asymmetry parameters, while deviations can arise from the discrete lattice, finite particle number, and stochastic fluctuations.

11

Temperature dependence

Increasing $\beta$ strengthens the influence of the potential relative to thermal fluctuations. At small $\beta$, the numerical current approaches zero, while larger $\beta$ values produce closer agreement with the analytical prediction.

12

Limitations and interpretation

The analytical solution provides a useful description of average transport behaviour, but does not reproduce every feature of the numerical model.

Key observation

Directed transport can emerge from stochastic thermal motion when spatial asymmetry is combined with time-dependent switching.

13

Conclusion

This project investigated stochastic particle motion in different potential landscapes, with particular focus on the flashing-ratchet mechanism for directed transport.

The simulations demonstrated how temperature, potential shape, asymmetry, and flashing time influence particle distributions and net current.

Comparing numerical simulations with an analytical expression showed agreement under suitable conditions while also illustrating the limitations of analytical approximations.
