Motor proteins transport molecular cargo within cells, using stochastic molecular motion to produce directed movement. This project investigates how a similar form of directed transport can emerge from a simple stochastic particle model.

Particles are represented as random walkers on a one-dimensional lattice. Their motion is influenced by the potential landscape and the temperature of the system. We first study how different potentials affect particle distributions, before introducing a periodically switched asymmetric potential.

Main question

How can stochastic thermal motion produce directed transport when interacting with an asymmetric potential landscape?

01

Stochastic Motion and Potential Landscapes

We begin with the diffusion equation, which describes the evolution of a particle distribution:

$$ \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} = D \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2}. $$

For an initial distribution $h(x)$, the solution is given by the Gaussian heat kernel:

$$ \phi(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(y) \exp\left( -\frac{(x-y)^2}{4Dt} \right) \,dy. $$

As $t\rightarrow0$, the Gaussian kernel approaches a Dirac delta distribution, recovering the initial condition $\phi(x,0)=h(x)$. This provides the theoretical starting point for our stochastic model.

In the numerical simulation, particles instead perform a discrete random walk. At each time step they can move left, remain in place, or move right. The probabilities are determined by the Boltzmann weights of the three possible positions:

$$ p^+ = \frac{e^{-\beta V(x+1)}} { e^{-\beta V(x-1)} + e^{-\beta V(x)} + e^{-\beta V(x+1)} } $$
$$ p^- = \frac{e^{-\beta V(x-1)}} { e^{-\beta V(x-1)} + e^{-\beta V(x)} + e^{-\beta V(x+1)} } $$
$$ p^0=1-p^+-p^-. $$

Here, $\beta=1/(k_BT)$ is the inverse temperature. At high temperature, thermal fluctuations dominate and the motion approaches an unbiased random walk. At low temperature, particles are more strongly influenced by the potential and preferentially occupy lower-energy states.

02

The Flashing Ratchet

The main part of the project is a flashing-ratchet model. The system alternates between a flat potential and an asymmetric periodic potential.

When the potential is flat, particles diffuse through thermal fluctuations. When the asymmetric potential is switched on, particles tend to move towards nearby minima. Repeating this process can create a net displacement even though each individual movement remains stochastic.

The cycle generates a net current.

The average current is measured from the difference between rightward and leftward particle movements:

$$ J= \frac{n^+-n^-}{N_p}. $$
03

Directed Transport

The asymmetry parameter $\alpha$ determines the shape of the sawtooth potential and therefore the direction of transport. Changing $\alpha$ changes which side of each potential well is steeper.

For the parameters used in our simulations, the current changes sign around $\alpha=0.5$. When $\alpha<0.5$ the current is positive, while $\alpha>0.5$ produces a negative current. At $\alpha=0.5$, the potential is symmetric and the net current approaches zero.

The switching time $T_p$ also affects the current. If the potential switches too quickly, particles have little time to respond. For intermediate values of $T_p$, the directed current becomes stronger, while for sufficiently long switching times the current decreases again in our simulations.

04

Numerical and Analytical Results

To validate the numerical model, we compare the simulated current with an analytical expression for the average current:

$$ J_{\mathrm{avg}} = \frac{N_x}{4T_p} \left[ \operatorname{erfc} \left( \frac{\alpha N_x}{2} \sqrt{\frac{3}{T_p}} \right) - \operatorname{erfc} \left( \frac{(1-\alpha)N_x}{2} \sqrt{\frac{3}{T_p}} \right) \right]. $$

The numerical and analytical results show good agreement for the dependence on $\alpha$, particularly in the low-temperature regime. Small deviations are expected because the simulation contains a finite number of particles and uses discrete positions and time steps.

Temperature enters the numerical model through $\beta$. At small $\beta$, thermal fluctuations dominate and the numerical current is strongly reduced. As $\beta$ increases, the potential has a stronger influence on the particle motion, and the numerical results approach the analytical prediction more closely.

Key observation

Random thermal motion can be converted into directed transport when spatial asymmetry and periodic switching break the symmetry of the system.

05

Conclusion

This project shows how stochastic particle motion can lead to directed transport. Starting from diffusion and a Boltzmann-weighted random walk, we investigated how different potential landscapes influence particle distributions.

The flashing-ratchet model demonstrated that combining thermal fluctuations with an asymmetric, periodically switched potential produces a net current. Both the direction and magnitude of this current depend on the asymmetry parameter, switching time and temperature.

The numerical simulations showed good agreement with the analytical prediction in the parameter regime investigated, while deviations highlighted the effects of finite particle number, stochastic fluctuations and the discrete numerical model.

Although the model is much simpler than a real molecular motor, it illustrates an important physical principle: random thermal motion can be rectified into directed transport when the symmetry of the system is appropriately broken.
